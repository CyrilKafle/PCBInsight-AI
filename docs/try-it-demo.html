How to read this

  1. The engine decides. Deterministic rules with named thresholds find every problem. No machine learning is involved in the score.
  2. The score is arithmetic. Each finding removes points based on its severity and how confident the rule is.
  3. The AI only explains. Claude receives a digest of the findings, never the raw board, and its citations are checked in code.
Known limits of this demo
  • The 0 to 100 score has no pass threshold. It summarizes deductions and is not a certification.
  • Documentation has no checks yet, so it reads 100 by default. A subscore of 100 means no deductions were found, not that the area was validated.
  • Dangling-end detection compares endpoints to pads, vias and traces within a position tolerance, so it can flag ends that are actually connected. That is why those findings carry low confidence.
  • Manufacturability checks currently cover trace width, annular ring, and via density.
  • Confidence values are set by hand per rule, not calibrated against labelled boards.

medium Thin power trace on net +3V3 (0.25mm) Power conf 0.65
PWR-001

+3V3

Why it matters

A trace this narrow limits current-carrying capacity and increases IR drop along the supply path, which can starve downstream components under load.

Size power trace width to the expected current, not the minimum fab rule.

Suggested fix

Widen this trace to at least 0.30mm, more for high-current rails.

medium Decoupling capacitor C1 is 3.2mm from U1 Decoupling conf 0.50
DECAP-001

U1C1+3V3

Why it matters

The nearest capacitor bridging +3V3 to ground is farther than recommended for a MCU, lengthening the loop the transient current has to travel and reducing the capacitor's effectiveness at high frequency.

Decoupling capacitors should sit as close as possible to the pin they protect.

Suggested fix

Move C1 within 3.0mm of U1, or add a closer one.

Glossary

Net
A set of pads that must be electrically connected, such as every pin that touches the 3.3 V supply.
Trace
A strip of copper that carries a signal or power between pads.
Via
A plated hole that moves a trace from one board layer to another.
Annular ring
The ring of copper left around a drilled hole. Too thin and a slightly off-centre drill breaks the connection.
Decoupling capacitor
A small capacitor placed right next to a chip's power pin to supply fast bursts of current.
Copper pour
A large filled area of copper, usually ground, that gives current an easy return path and helps spread heat.
Differential pair
Two traces that carry one signal as a voltage difference, like USB D+ and D-. They should match in length and shape.
Confidence
A fixed per-rule estimate from 0 to 1 of how likely a finding is real. It is not a statistical probability.

PCB Review Summary — stm32_usb_dev

Overall score: 98/100 — a strong result. No critical or high-severity issues were found; the deterministic analysis surfaced only 2 medium and 6 low-severity items across a 4-layer, 18-component board.

Recurring Theme: Possible Dangling Trace Stubs

Six of the eight findings (RTE-001 through RTE-006) share the identical pattern — a trace endpoint that doesn't clearly terminate at a pad, via, or same-net trace within tolerance. These span several functionally distinct nets: USB differential pair (/USB_DP, /USB_DM), crystal lines (/XTAL_IN, /XTAL_OUT), and SWD programming/debug lines (/SWDIO, /SWCLK).

  • All six carry the same low severity and the same confidence of 0.4, which is below the 0.5 threshold — these should be treated as unverified and manually checked, not as confirmed defects. It's plausible some or all are false positives from endpoint-detection tolerance rather than actual unterminated stubs.
  • If genuine, stubs on /USB_DP and /USB_DM are worth checking first among this group, since USB differential signaling is more sensitive to impedance discontinuities than the crystal or SWD lines, though no signal-integrity issue was actually flagged for these nets (Signal Integrity subscore is 100).
  • The common recommendation across all six is consistent: verify the endpoint lands on a pad, then extend or remove the stub as needed.

Power Delivery Observations

Two medium-severity findings, both related to power delivery robustness:

  • PWR-001 (confidence 0.65): the +3V3 rail includes a 0.25mm-wide trace segment, flagged as thin relative to current-carrying needs. This is the higher-confidence item in the batch and warrants attention — widening to at least 0.30mm is the supplied recommendation.
  • DECAP-001 (confidence 0.5): decoupling capacitor C1 sits 3.2mm from U1 on +3V3, slightly beyond the recommended proximity for effective high-frequency decoupling at the MCU pin. At exactly the 0.5 confidence boundary, this is plausible but should still be confirmed against the actual layout before committing to a fix.
